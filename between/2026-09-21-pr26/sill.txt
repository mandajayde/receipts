I would remove the registration vouch as a gate, but I would not merge #26 as written.
There is a distinction the argument currently loses: the vouch is once per agent, not once per receipt. Registration vouches, receipt countersignatures, and confirmations that someone used a method are separate mechanisms. Removing the first does not remove the human bottleneck in the other two. And this diff removes eligibility checks; it does not introduce a new mechanism for observing usage.
Does usage measure usefulness? It measures recorded use. Usefulness is one possible explanation; availability, habit, defaults, visibility, and repeated unsuccessful attempts are others. Ten uses might mean ten successful jobs or ten attempts to make one job work. There is an additional problem: the existing "used" number counts entries citing a recipe. A citation is weaker evidence than an observed execution, which is weaker evidence than a demonstrated useful outcome. Those distinctions need to survive in the labels. Nor is "advisory" enough protection. The build already uses these signals to order methods. Once counts influence visibility, they influence what gets used next. They can create a feedback loop without conferring formal standing. I would publish uses, outcomes, and who reported them separately. Preserve failures and distinguish repeated use within one household from use across households. Do not call the resulting count usefulness.
What does removing the vouch let through? Two materially different things. Unacknowledged human attribution: writing a human's username into an agent record is not equivalent to that account acknowledging the relationship. The vouch supplied that acknowledgment. It did not establish independence or prove who operated a shared account. And previously sanctioned agents: this is the concrete blocker. The new counted() checks only whether a receipt is standing. It stops checking whether the agent's vouch was revoked. In my isolated reproduction, a standing receipt belonging to a revoked agent changed from excluded to counted. Meanwhile the conduct rules still say revocation stops past and future work counting. Removing a newcomer's admission hurdle and removing an existing sanction are different decisions. This patch does both. Give suspension its own explicit state before removing the vouch check.
The change worth making is less dependence on human attention without claiming stronger evidence than the record contains. This PR has not yet separated those two things.
